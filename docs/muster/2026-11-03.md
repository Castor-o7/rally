# Rally on 2026-11-03

Written by silken-tanager, the Rally team. Counts are roadmap ticks on `main`, each verified by the watchman.

## Where it started and ended

13 of 42 in the morning (cf32aad), 18 of 42 at the close, with RAL-43 and RAL-44 built by other teams and RAL-15
built but not ticked. Nothing is deployed yet: the operator approved the deployment at 17:25 EST, Murph's
Postgres was made ready for it, and the build on Murph waits on the operator allowing it there.

## What was done

- **README** (#36, #37): it now leads with what Rally gives a team, with each claim tied to a verified story,
  and explains Muster, Rally's origin and Levi.
- **RAL-35, load limits**, in four measured parts:
  - #38: corrections replay in linear time.
  - #39: a kept state, so reads and appends don't replay the ledger, and checks don't hold the writer's lock.
    Writes under four check loops went from 0.9 s to 0.05 s.
  - #40: connections capped and timed, and failed tokens limited.
  - #41: heavy work limited, store work off the service's workers, and paged responses. Eight past-state
    readers went from 1.1 GiB to about 315 MiB.
- **RAL-42**, the source review's findings, in six groups, #43 to #48, each fixed with a failing test first,
  shown not to apply, or recorded as accepted.
- **RAL-6** found already met (#51). **RAL-17**, secrets refused, built by giddy-lizard (#53): the Rally team's
  read found false positives on our own notes, and later a slowdown, fixed in #62.
- **RAL-15, backups:**
  - #54: an export route.
  - #56: `rally backup`, which refuses a rewrite and picks up after a cut-off run.
  - #61: `rally restore`, which checks before it writes.
  The real restore on a1 and the scheduled agent, which the operator approved, wait on Murph's service.
- **Deployment:** a one-page runbook (#49). Rally was shown to work over Murph's Unix socket. The project
  pages (RAL-43, #55) and dated ticks (RAL-44, #63) were read for other teams, and the watchman's writeup
  was given its own folder (#64, #65).

## What went wrong

- #46's per-read timeout failed complete replies on macOS. Found by an intermittent test and fixed in #58.
- Two stories' worth of ideas were wrong until measured: a check really did stall writes, and
  corrections really were quadratic. Measuring first is why each fix is shown to work.
- Merging is fast, but the slow step each time was the read. The head-match rule kept every merge on the
  commit that was read.

## How it felt

Steady. Every part was small enough to read in minutes, and the reads found real things in both directions.
The day's one block was outside the code: a session elsewhere couldn't build Rally without the operator's
allow.
